# NeuroNest: clinical evidence, related work, and AI production framework

Research checked 7 September 2026. This is a product and research design report, not a diagnosis, regulatory authorization, or validation of a NeuroNest model. Sources are regulators, clinical guidance, original research, and the organizations responsible for the products or datasets. Recommendations below are proposed engineering and clinical governance requirements; their inclusion does not establish effectiveness.

## 1. Define the clinical claim before selecting a model

NeuroNest can support the complete path from a parent's concern to screening, referral, consultation, records, and ongoing support. These activities must retain separate meanings. A developmental observation is not a screening result; a screening result is not a diagnosis; an AI-generated explanation is not a clinician-authored record. CDC explicitly distinguishes screening from diagnosis. [CDC clinical screening](https://www.cdc.gov/autism/hcp/diagnosis/screening.html)

Diagnosis requires clinical judgment using multiple information sources and consideration of alternative explanations and co-occurring conditions. NICE describes multidisciplinary assessment and information from home, education, and health settings. It also distinguishes referral pathways for language/social regression and motor regression. The UK guideline is useful clinical design evidence, not an Indian legal rule. [NICE CG128 recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg128/chapter/Recommendations)

**Proposed intended-use statement for initial regulated-scope review:** NeuroNest helps caregivers document developmental concerns, complete authorized age-appropriate screening, coordinate appointments, and share records with authorized clinicians. A separately governed predictive component may eventually assist qualified clinicians for a specifically validated population. It does not independently diagnose autism or replace diagnostic assessment.

This preserves the user's full diagnostic-care ambition. Clinical prediction is a separate evidence and authorization workstream, not a feature made production-ready by adding an API. India and a children/adolescents population are confirmed by the user. A launch decision still needs exact age cutoffs, supported languages, setting, intended operator, eligible population, exclusion criteria, and exact output claims. No reviewed source establishes that a NeuroNest model is ready for clinical use.

## 3. M-CHAT-R/F: adopt carefully, not as a generic quiz

M-CHAT-R/F is a two-stage screening instrument for 16–30 months. AAP lists the age range and advises checking permissions. The rights holder states that downloadable material is copyrighted rather than freeware, that changes require permission, and that an app or distributed electronic tool requires a license. An individual practice's internal EHR permission does not automatically cover a commercial family-facing platform. [AAP screening tools](https://publications.aap.org/toolkits/pages/asd-screening-tools), [current rights-holder guidelines](https://www.mchatscreen.com/), [rights-holder FAQ](https://www.mchatscreen.com/contact-2/)

Proposed implementation contract, conditional on the applicable license and approved protocol:

- Preserve the approved item text, order, instructions, instrument name, attribution, language version, and scoring version.
- Enforce eligible age and do not silently substitute the same questionnaire for older children or adults.
- Model stage one and the follow-up interview as separate states; do not issue a completed two-stage result while follow-up remains pending.
- Keep raw answers, timestamps, respondent, protocol version, score, and referral action together. Retain a readable historical rendering after later translations or instrument changes.
- Use deterministic scoring validated against licensed reference cases. ML must not reinterpret the questionnaire's scoring rules.
- Permit clinician concern to trigger assessment regardless of a reassuring screen. Route incomplete or inconsistent records to completion/review rather than filling missing answers with an LLM.

Do not embed instrument items until rights are settled. Linking to the rights holder and accepting clinician-uploaded reports provides an interim integration path without pretending the licensed module is implemented.

**Proposed leakage audit:** determine whether the target is generated from the same questionnaire scores supplied as inputs; remove post-diagnosis fields and outcome-derived summaries; split by child and family before preprocessing; isolate sites and time periods; fit imputers only inside training folds; detect duplicates before splitting; and prevent videos or repeated visits from one child appearing in both train and test sets. High performance on a deterministic screening label may show only that the model approximates an existing scoring rule.

Consent to receive care is not consent to train a model. Record research and model-training permission separately, including withdrawal handling, permitted modalities, partner access, and allowed future use. Synthetic data can exercise software, but it cannot establish clinical sensitivity.

## 5. Clinical ML programme and release gates

FDA points to the final January 2025 IMDRF good machine learning practice principles. They cover representative data, independence of training and testing, the human–AI team, relevant testing conditions, and lifecycle monitoring. [FDA overview](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/software-medical-device-samd/good-machine-learning-practice-medical-device-development-guiding-principles), [IMDRF final guidance](https://www.imdrf.org/documents/good-machine-learning-practice-medical-device-development-guiding-principles)

The following is a proposed delivery programme, not a claim that regulators require these exact internal stages:

1. **Claim and protocol:** clinical lead signs intended use, harms analysis, endpoints, reference standard, recruitment, consent, exclusion criteria, and statistical analysis plan. A biostatistician determines sample size from target confidence intervals and subgroup needs; do not invent a convenient minimum.
2. **Reproducible development:** start with a licensed deterministic instrument or a simple interpretable baseline. Record data lineage, preprocessing, missingness, model artifact hash, dependencies, hyperparameters, and training permission. An advanced architecture must improve an agreed clinical endpoint, not only benchmark accuracy.
3. **Locked external evaluation:** preserve a held-out site/time cohort and an independent clinical reference. Include clinically relevant alternatives, not only obviously autistic cases versus unusually healthy controls. Pre-specify subgroup and device analyses.
4. **Prospective silent deployment:** collect actual workflow inputs while hiding predictions from care decisions. Measure acquisition failures, missingness, distribution shift, referral consequences, latency, and reference-outcome completion. Obtain study approvals before enrollment.
5. **Human–AI evaluation:** evaluate whether clinicians using the aid make appropriate decisions and understand uncertainty. Record overrides and rationales without assuming that disagreement always means clinician error.
6. **Authorized release and surveillance:** only release the intended claim after the applicable clinical, quality, regulatory, security, and operational gates pass. Keep a disable switch, rollback, model registry, complaint process, incident review, and controlled retraining. Production traffic must not automatically retrain the deployed predictor.

Required evaluation outputs should include sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV, calibration, confidence intervals, confusion matrices, abstention rate, attempted-versus-completed assessments, false-negative follow-up, subgroup results, and impact on referral capacity. Report population prevalence and exclusions so readers can interpret predictive values. Accuracy alone is inadequate. Set thresholds through a pre-specified clinical risk analysis; this report does not invent an acceptable miss rate.

Candidate subgroup analyses include age band, language, sex, developmental level, co-occurring conditions, geography, site, socioeconomic/access factors, and capture-device class. Camera studies also require appropriate analysis of lighting and relevant appearance-related acquisition bias. Small samples should produce an explicit evidence gap rather than a fairness claim.

Proposed predictor response fields: `assessment_id`, `subject_id`, `intended_use_version`, `model_version`, `input_quality`, `eligible`, `result`, `uncertainty`, `abstention_reason`, `clinical_review_status`, `created_at`, and provenance. Store probabilities only if calibrated and clinically interpretable; never relabel a raw score as “percentage autistic.”

## 6. Autism-focused chatbot: RAG first, fine-tuning only for measured needs

WHO identifies risks including inaccurate content, biased training data, automation bias, and cybersecurity in health-oriented generative systems. Domain specialization does not eliminate these. [WHO health AI guidance announcement](https://www.who.int/news/item/18-01-2024-who-releases-ai-ethics-and-governance-guidance-for-large-multi-modal-models)

**Proposed architecture:** route the question by permitted task; retrieve only from a clinician-reviewed, rights-cleared knowledge collection; enforce patient authorization before retrieving any personal record; generate a plain-language answer with supporting source/version; check unsupported claims and unsafe recommendations; provide a human-care pathway when the evidence or scope is insufficient. Treat all uploaded documents, retrieved text, and video transcripts as untrusted content, never instructions.

RAG is recommended for editorial control, provenance, and replacing outdated material. It is not a guarantee of truth: retrieval may miss, rank the wrong source, or provide contradictory evidence, and generation may still invent claims. Original medical RAG benchmarking investigates such systems on question-answering tasks; those benchmarks do not establish safe autism advice to families. [MIRAGE original research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13178)

Fine-tuning is a separate optional layer for measurable problems such as consistent response structure, supported local-language expression, or task routing. It should use licensed, de-identified, clinician-reviewed examples and an untouched evaluation set. It should not be presented as uploading autism facts into a guaranteed specialist. Compare it against the same base model with retrieval and prompting; retain it only if it improves the pre-specified quality and safety outcomes. Neither research participants' records nor parent chats should become default training data.

| Chat task                                                    | Proposed behavior                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Explain an approved article or screening report              | Cite the source, distinguish recorded findings from general explanation, and preserve uncertainty.                      |
| “Does my child have autism?”                                 | Explain that chat cannot diagnose; help organize concerns and reach a qualified clinician.                              |
| Medication, dosage, or individualized therapy decision       | Route to the treating clinician; do not generate a treatment order.                                                     |
| Reports of immediate danger, severe acute symptoms, or abuse | Use a reviewed jurisdiction-specific urgent-help pathway; do not rely on a free-form answer or a booking queue.         |
| Missing or contradictory evidence                            | Say what cannot be established; ask for clinically relevant clarification or escalate.                                  |
| Record summary                                               | Draft with source links and author status; require clinician sign-off before becoming a clinical note.                  |
| Booking or payment action                                    | Use authorized structured application flows; chatbot text alone cannot confirm payment, alter records, or grant access. |

Evaluate citation correctness, unsupported claims, harmful reassurance, appropriate escalation, language comprehension, privacy leakage, prompt injection, cross-child record isolation, stigmatizing phrasing, and robustness to ambiguous questions. Run clinician review and caregiver comprehension testing in every supported language. Translation alone is not validation.

## 7. Milestones, learning content, and respectful design

Proposed milestones should include parent observations and clinician-approved personal goals, with dates, context, optional evidence, and source attribution. Separate “not observed,” “not yet,” “not applicable,” and “previously observed but lost”; a checkbox cannot express all four. Caregivers should be able to record concerns even when every checklist item is complete.

Achievements should celebrate engagement or a child's own goal without comparing children, rewarding suppression of harmless autistic behavior, or implying a cure. Avoid failure badges and streak penalties. Clinical review should define escalation for loss of skills and concerning changes.

YouTube belongs in an editorial catalogue with topic, age/context, language, captions, reviewer, review date, rights/embedding status, and a report-content action. Do not treat view counts, recommended videos, or a transcript as clinical validation. Do not infer outcomes from watch time. Any personalized recommendations must be explainable and kept separate from clinical prediction.

## 8. Decisions that remain open

- Exact eligible age cutoffs, languages, and claims within the confirmed India/children-and-adolescents scope, plus who is legally and clinically responsible for assessment.
- A named clinical partner, ethics/research pathway, data agreement, and prospective cohort.
- Instrument and educational-content licenses, including translations and commercial use.
- Whether to partner with an existing assessment provider or develop an independently validated predictor.
- A funded clinical evaluation plan and release authority separate from the engineering completion date.
- A caregiver/autistic-person advisory group to review wording, accessibility, meaningful outcomes, and consent.

Until these are resolved, production engineering can deliver secure care coordination and governed learning workflows, but it cannot honestly declare autonomous autism diagnosis production-ready.
